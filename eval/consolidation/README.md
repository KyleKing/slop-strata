# Consolidation dataset

What `x/new-duplicate`, `duplicate_groups`, `split_candidates`, `case_sets`, `jsx_groups`, `sibling_sets`, and `literal_groups` were designed and measured against: commits in public repositories that replaced repeated code with one function, constant, or type, each read by hand, the pairs of look-alike fragments nobody merged, and the hand labels behind the tables in [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md) sections 8 and 9.

Everything here is data and Python. No Go file lives under `eval/`, so the build, the tests, and the linters do not read it.

## Fields

`events.jsonl`:

- `gid, repo, lang` (`py`, `ts`, or `go`), `sha, parent, commit_ts` (unix), `subj, nfiles_in_commit`
- `kind` is `extract` (a shared helper, constant, type alias, or component), `parameterise`, `pullup`, or `other`, which the note explains
- `note` is a one-line description by the reader, `confidence` is `clear` or `borderline`, and `partial_diff` is set when the reader saw a truncated diff
- `detector_kinds` and `stratum` say which structural family surfaced the candidate
- `sites[]` are the repeated sites at `parent`: `path`, `qual` (the qualified name of the function or method), `unit_lines`, `region_lines` (the removed region, in parent line numbers), `ntok`, `nstmt`
- `target` is what replaced them: `name, path, lines` (at the commit), `kind, from_detector`. The name was corrected by hand where the detector resolved it wrongly
- `n_sites, ntok_min, ntok_median, ntok_max, nstmt_min, nstmt_median, same_file, same_dir, cross_package, test_only, any_test`
- Similarity of the best pair of sites before the merge: `sim_pair_max_raw` (a sequence-matcher ratio on raw tokens), `sim_pair_max_shape` (the same with identifiers and literals normalised), `sim_pair_max_jacc5` (Jaccard over token 5-grams), `holes_best_pair` (aligned positions where the shape is equal and the identifier or literal differs), `unaligned_best_pair` (tokens outside the alignment), and `sim_best_pair`, the full record
- `hist` comes from `git blame -w -M` at `parent` over each site's region, six sites at most: `birth_spread_days`, `dup_lifetime_days`, `oldest_site_age_days`, `same_birth_commit`, `later_site_after_earlier`, `same_author_as_earlier`, `lockstep_commits`, `solo_edit_commits`, `diverged`, `cochange_commits`, `prior_commits_per_file`, `prior_commits_12m_per_file`. Blame sees only surviving lines, so the edit counts are lower bounds, and a formatting commit can overwrite a birth date
- `message_flags, censored_for_controls, n_controls, sibling_class_methods`

`rejected.jsonl`: `gid, repo, sha, parent, verdict` (`R` or `U`), `reason, note, detector_kinds, stratum, n_sites, subj`. The reasons include `spurious`, `mirror`, `api_change`, `move`, `rename`, `test_rewrite`, `base_class_swap`, `inverse`, and `deprecation_shim`.

`controls.jsonl` has the fields of an event for one pair of regions (`n_sites` is 2) sampled at the event's `parent` in the same repository: statement-aligned body fragments on the event's side of the test split, within 0.6 to 1.6 times the event's median site size, and within 0.15 of its best pair's shape similarity. Eight are `match_quality: relaxed`, at 0.4 to 2.5 times and 0.30. A pair is kept only when the parent is 24 months or more before the clone's commit, both units still exist 24 months later with 80% of the region intact (`still_present_at_followup`), and neither unit is a site of any structural candidate in those 24 months. It also carries `event_gid, holes, unaligned`, and `label: negative`.

`labels/duplicate-groups.tsv` and `labels/split-candidates.tsv`: repository, language, rank in the list, verdict, note. `labels/split-candidates-v2.tsv` adds the file and lines of the block, and `carried` when the first list named the same block and the verdict was copied from it, or `fresh` when the block was read for this file. `labels/new-duplicate-*.tsv` have a header row. `g`, `s`, `p`, and `tok` in the second are the kind of hit (`run`, `join`, `expr`), its score, its parameters, and its tokens, as an instrumented build printed them, and `key_suffix` is `was` on two rows that label a finding as an earlier build worded it.

`labels/split-candidates-v3.tsv` has the seven columns of the second file. The rank is the candidate's place in the list of the rules as shipped, or `-` when it is in no top five of that list and was read for another reason. The last column says which: `carried` (39) is a block of the twelve that the second file also names, with its verdict and note copied, `tuned` (30) a block of the twelve read while the three rules were being chosen, 21 of them in a final top five and 9 that only a rule set that was tried and dropped put there, `fresh` (43) the top five of the ten held-out repositories, read once after the rules were frozen, and `fresh-before` (7) the candidates the rules before these had in a top five of the ten and the shipped rules do not, read in the same sitting.

`labels/case-sets.tsv`: repository, language, rank in the list, verdict, note. A case-set verdict is `yes` (a maintainer would plausibly tie the places together with a table, a type, or an exhaustive match, or at least wants to know they are parallel), `plausible`, or `no`. `labels/new-duplicate-*.tsv` have a header row. `g`, `s`, `p`, and `tok` in the second are the kind of hit (`run`, `join`, `expr`), its score, its parameters, and its tokens, as an instrumented build printed them, and `key_suffix` is `was` on two rows that label a finding as an earlier build worded it.

`labels/jsx-groups.tsv`: repository, rank in the list, verdict, note. A JSX-group verdict is `yes` (a maintainer would plausibly make the block one component, or render it from one list), `plausible` (a real repeat whose extraction is a judgment call: a loading skeleton, a layout a component library dictates, a pair that differs in most of its values), or `no`.

`labels/sibling-sets.tsv`: repository, language, rank in the list, verdict, note. A sibling-set verdict is `yes` (a maintainer would plausibly give the sites a shared base class, a generic, or a table of what differs), `plausible` (the sites are written alike and are kept apart for a reason a maintainer could defend: a client free of dependencies, a route file per resource, a frozen version), or `no` (parallel on purpose: two flavours of one public API, a file per locale).

`labels/dup-literals.tsv`: repository, `fit` or `fresh`, `top` or `draw`, rank in the list, verdict, note. `fit` marks the 15 repositories the rules of the list were chosen on and `fresh` the 13 no rule was fitted to. `top` is one of a repository's first five groups and `draw` one drawn from the ranks below. The note opens with the group's value in double quotes. A literal-group verdict is `yes` (a maintainer would plausibly give the value one name, or read the constant that already names it), `plausible` (a real repeat of one load-bearing value whose naming is a judgment call: a header name, a key a protocol defines), or `no`.

`labels/change-rules.tsv`: repository, commit, rule, the path and line the finding was reported at, verdict, note. A row is one finding of `scripts/replay_change.py`, read in the commit that made it beside the site or sites it names. The verdict is `yes` (the author wrote a copy or a parallel place where reusing or extending the one that was there was the better move, and a reviewer would want to be told), `plausible` (the repeat or the parallel is real and there is a reason the places are apart), or `no`, and a `no` opens with its reason: `sub-router`, `key factory`, `payload record`, `interface as an object`, `refactor`, `chance words`, `chance shape`, `library skeleton`, `edit`, `intentional mirror`, `idiom`, `generated`, `reformat`, `rewrite of both sites`, `test support`, `chance`. A note says `unified later` where a later commit was seen to merge the sites, and says so where the record rule that came of these labels no longer reports the finding. The rows are in the order of the rules, then by repository (the twelve, the ten, and the six applications, each by name), then newest commit first.

`labels/jsx-recall.tsv`: repository, commit, the component the commit extracted, how many sites it merged, whether a JSX group held two of them at the commit's parent (`yes` or `no`), the group's rank in the parent's list, the lowest floor of elements at which a build finds two sites in one group (`6` is the shipped floor, `3, gate off` a floor of 3 with the hole gate lifted, `-` none), why it was missed, and a note. A site is a hunk of the parent that lost markup and gained a use of the component. The reasons are `small` (the repeated block is under 6 elements at every site), `structure` (the sites did one job with different markup), `near-miss` (one tag or one attribute name apart), `conditional` (a ternary between elements too small to be a block), `wrapper` (a frame around bodies that share nothing), `path` (a directory the pass leaves out), and `drawing` (an `svg`).

## How the numbers were produced

The mining pipeline, in `scripts/`:

1. `units.py` reads functions, methods, constants, and classes out of a file with tree-sitter, each with its own tokens, comments and nested units left out
2. `detect.py REPO OUT` walks every non-merge commit, skips one that touches more than 30 files or no source file, and compares each unit's tokens at the parent and at the commit. It writes candidates of three kinds: `extract`, `pullup`, and `merge_delete`
3. `group.py` merges the candidates of one commit that share a site, and `mkqueue.py` shuffles them into a review queue per stratum with a fixed seed
4. A person read the queue, with `view2.py` to print each candidate's sites, and wrote `labels/verdicts.tsv`
5. `build_events.py`, `run_hist.py` with `age.py`, `controls.py`, `run_hist_ctl.py`, and `finalize.py` write the three data files, and `analyse.py` writes `analysis.txt`

`scripts/setup.sh WORK` lays out a work directory outside this repository and clones each repository at its commit. The scripts read and write beside themselves (`WORK/data`, `WORK/repos`), which is why they run from a copy. They ran under Python 3.13 with tree-sitter 0.27.0, tree-sitter-go 0.25.0, tree-sitter-python 0.25.0, tree-sitter-typescript 0.23.2. The per-repository candidate files and the grouped candidates are not kept here: they are 18 MB and `detect.py` and `group.py` write them again.

## Known limits

- The change-rule labels are one reader's, made from the lines a finding names and the first site it points at, a dozen lines of each, with the commit's subject and file counts. A finding that names ten sites was read against one. The record rule was fitted to four of the rows, which the notes of those rows say
- The draw of `x/new-duplicate` is two per repository, so it says how often a finding is right in a typical repository and not how many of all its findings are: over a quarter of them are in documenso, which has two rows
- Whether a later commit merged the sites was looked at for a few findings of the two rarer rules whose site was gone at the clone's head, by reading the log of the file. Two were merged and the others were not traced. Most of the commits are under a year old
- The extraction commits were found by the shape of a diff: a component file added and markup replaced by its use in two files of the same commit. An extraction into an existing file, one done over several commits, and one in a commit of more than 60 files are not there, and 24 of the 35 are from one application, plane. One person kept or set aside each candidate and named each miss
- A site of an extraction is a hunk, so a group that covers two hunks of one file counts, and a group folded from several blocks is matched on the lines of its first

- Most verdicts were made from a view of the removed regions, the head of the target, and the identifier the sites gained. The full diff was read for a minority
- One person read every candidate, every finding, and every group
- 36 events are less than 24 months old and have no controls, and 27 older ones found no pair that matched. The controls are mostly structural boilerplate, and a pair nobody merged in the 24 months may still be one somebody should have
- The detector finds a consolidation by the code it removes, so the dataset holds none found by repeated conditions, and its precision differs by stratum: 73% of the reviewed resolved extractions were true, and 11% to 21% of the other three
- The guards `duplicate_groups` added (stand-alone directories, tables of values) were chosen on the twelve repositories the group labels come from, so those labels overstate what the list does elsewhere
- The split rules were chosen on the twelve repositories the first split labels come from, and the second list was read by the person who chose them, who knew which rule had kept each candidate. The 70% there overstates the list. The 40 `fresh` rows are the ones no rule was fitted to
- The three later split rules were chosen on the twelve as well, so the 75% there overstates them the same way, and the 43 `fresh` rows of the third file are the measure. They are one person's reading, the person who chose the rules, of ten libraries, and of 43 candidates where 50 were hoped for: swr and zustand list 3 between them. A verdict that leans on a count (an output the tool did not count) was made from the code and not from the count
- The case-set labels use a wider `yes` than the group labels use `real`: a set of places that are parallel and that nothing ties together counts when a maintainer would want to know of it, whether or not a merge is the answer. Sixteen of the 30 fresh rows are one repository, date-fns, and fourteen of those are one design, a formatter and a parser that each switch over the tokens of one unit
- The literal-group labels are one reader's, made from the constant and up to five places of each group, the uses first, and not from every place. The `fit` rows overstate the list, as its rules were chosen on them. `plausible` is wide there: it holds every header name and protocol key, which are 17 of the 22 on the `fresh` rows, and a reader who calls those `no` gets 34% and 44% in place of 34% and 78%. Six of the 50 `fresh` rows are Go and five of those are one repository. Nothing was mined for the list, so the labels say nothing of what it misses
- The JSX-group labels are one reader's on five applications, each read from the first and the last site of a group. They cover the top of each list and a draw from the rest of its first 20, and nothing past rank 20. Nothing was mined for them, so they say nothing of what the list misses
- The sibling-set labels are one reader's, made from the first two or three shared members of a set at up to four of its sites, and on 34 sets in all, because the pass lists few: a per-language or per-rank number from them is a handful of rows. The 15 library rows were read before the last guard was chosen. `yes` asks whether a maintainer would plausibly unify the sites, and no maintainer was asked. Nothing was mined for them, so they say nothing of what the list misses
